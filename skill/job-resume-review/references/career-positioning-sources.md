# Career positioning and search visibility: source extracts

Paraphrased from the public post text, Danilo's attached one-page prompt, and
LinkedIn's documentation on 2026-10-08. These local extracts preserve the
relevant source content if the originals change or disappear. URLs provide
attribution. The diagnostic rules remain in `./references/rubric.md`.

## Danilo Moraes: career positioning

Source: [post and attached career-analysis prompt](https://lnkd.in/p/du3Wxf6J),
“Análise Estratégia da Minha Carreira.”

The prompt starts with the CV and asks for search difficulties, current or
last role, residence and work preferences, and intended roles or industries.
It requests a candid recruiter-style reading rather than generic praise:

- Explain possible reasons the search is producing disappointing results.
- Assess realistic seniority and suitable company types and sizes.
- Identify a small set of target job titles and industries.
- Compare the current profile with an intended future role and identify gaps.
- Propose three immediate actions for the CV, LinkedIn, and job search.

The original also requests regional compensation research and a five-to-seven
year development plan; those parts are outside this review's scope.

The post's screening percentages describe the author's reported experience,
not validated universal fit thresholds. The prompt frames AI analysis as a
starting point for discussion with someone who knows the target market,
rather than a verdict on the candidate.

## Jessica B.: terminology in sourcing

Source: [technical-sourcing post](https://lnkd.in/p/dupUPeFQ).

The author describes actively sourcing technical candidates with Boolean
searches and promotes examining a professional profile from the searcher's
perspective. The useful idea is to inspect whether relevant role and
capability terminology is recognizable on the profile, rather than assessing
only the CV sent with applications.

This is practitioner guidance in a promotional post, not a measured study of
recruiter discovery. It does not establish that portals have stopped working,
that exact wording is always necessary, that missing wording proves a profile
is excluded from search, or that profile edits produce a particular salary
or hiring outcome.

## LinkedIn Recruiter: documented Boolean controls

Source: [Use Boolean to filter search results in Recruiter and Recruiter Lite](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a415295).

The documentation describes filters for titles, locations, companies, skills,
schools, industries, and languages. OR includes alternatives, AND combines
conditions, and NOT excludes terms. Parentheses group terms; quoted searches
and Boolean modifiers are supported in Job titles, Companies, and Keywords.

Keyword matches can be highlighted in profile summaries, experience, and
skills. The documentation recommends including related titles and essential
skills. It also notes that Keywords ignores some stop words, so literal
phrase presence is not a complete model of search behavior.

This supports an illustrative terminology query across professional fields.
It does not establish indexing or ranking for a particular profile, guaranteed
discovery, or whether a listed capability is demonstrated by the candidate's
work.
