# Career positioning and search visibility: rules

Apply these with the Career positioning and Recruiter search visibility
sections of `./references/rubric.md` whenever either runs. They add to those
sections and do not repeat them.

## Career positioning

- Be direct and realistic: give the reading a senior recruiter would give,
  without generic praise.
- Start with an honest diagnosis of why the search is probably not producing
  the expected results, each reason tied to a CV location or supplied
  context. Say which reasons the CV cannot settle.
- Place the realistic level in a market context: the company types and
  sizes where the candidate is most competitive today.
- Name concrete job titles to search for within each role direction.
- When the user names a target role above the current level (for example
  Director or VP), state what is missing to reach it.
- Among the immediate actions, cover repositioning the CV and LinkedIn
  profile and how the search itself is run.
- Keep compensation research and multi-year development plans out of the
  review.
- Treat screening rates or fit percentages from recruiter anecdotes, such as
  "most CVs fall below 50% fit", as one person's experience, never as
  thresholds to score against.
- Present the result as a starting point, not a verdict: it does not replace
  a conversation with someone who knows the target market.

## Recruiter search visibility

- Read each professional surface the way a sourcer running Boolean searches
  would: can a search for the role and capability terms find this profile
  without the candidate applying?
- On LinkedIn, check the places recruiter search matches keywords: the
  headline/profile card, About summary, each experience's title,
  description, and location, and Skills.
- Build the illustrative query the way LinkedIn Recruiter supports it:
  uppercase AND, OR, NOT; parentheses to group; quotes for exact phrases in
  job titles, companies, and keywords. "+" and "-" are unsupported. Filters
  include job titles, location, companies, skills, schools, industries, and
  spoken languages.
- Keep the query specific and inclusive, built from related job titles and
  essential skills rather than long exclusion lists; overlong queries
  degrade results.
- Keyword search ignores stop words such as "and", "of", "for", "with",
  "after", so "after sales" matches "sales". A term's distinctive words
  carry the match; exact phrase presence is not a complete model of search.
- Do not repeat promotional claims as fact: that portals or applications no
  longer work, that recruiters stopped reading CVs, that a profile is
  invisible unless it matches an exact syntax, or that wording changes
  produce a salary or hiring outcome.
